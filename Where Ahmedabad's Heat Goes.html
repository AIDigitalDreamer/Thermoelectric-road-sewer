The check that convinced me

68.2 against 68

An independent Indian study built pavement temperature contours from air temperature at 37 stations, and reports about 68 °C for the critical Indian zone. My equation gives 68.2 °C for Ahmedabad in May.

Two completely separate routes, 0.2 °C apart. That was the moment I started trusting these numbers.

The one number nobody has measured

How much does the heat pipe matter?

Everything between the road and the sewage resists heat flow, and the module only generates from the share of the difference that lands across itself. The heat pipe is the biggest unknown in that stack. I assumed 0.020 — and nobody, including me, has measured it.

Reality check

Does it still hold?

My paper does not really claim a wattage. It needs a 100 µW sensor and a 15 W fan. Those are what matter.

What I would change

In the paper

Report a range, not a single figure. Replace the 20 °C sewage assumption with the measured Indian data, and say why the European sources were the wrong ones to reach for.

Then lead with the requirement test rather than the wattage — because the wattage is uncertain by a factor of nine and the conclusion is not. And say plainly that the heat pipe is unmeasured. That is the argument for the bench test being the next thing I do.
